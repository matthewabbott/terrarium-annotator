`lift`, `mine shaft`, `private section`, `layered armor`, `magical experiments`, `headsman` — generic-descriptor texture. These are the graveyard's intake; all are quote-grounded so culling is safe.

### Systemic patterns visible
1. **"Revision:" gloss prefix** — several card glosses start with "Revision: …" (the update-narrowing issue in the wild; rename/revert ops now exist to repair).
2. **Nickname/surname linking is a metric AND product gap**: `mik`↔`Mikhael Abdul-Hakim`, `suresh`↔`Archmagos Megalos`, `ring-of-sanity`↔`Emperor's Hand signet` — the entities exist; the short-form aliases don't. Alias harvesting (from gold labels + in-text short forms) is cheap researcher work.
3. **Spelling variants**: `bhatak`/`bhataki`, `Shahzadi`/`Shahzada` — merge-queue material (do NOT auto-merge; same-name-different-thing is real in this story).

- True capture holes: books (prompt callout), side characters (admission prompt: "name every named human your batch introduces"), taxonomy nodes (sub-entry rule at write time).

Author statement (out-of-story mechanics answer), upgrading Animate from a name-only catalog entry to a known function.
- **Confuse** — A metamagic effect named in the 'Utility Effects' tome in the Academy library's private section, listed alongside Mark, Animate, Create, and Sleep. No usage details are known yet — only that the effect exists as a named entry in the metamagic catalog.
- **Shahzadi** — A title for a woman of the royal family (the Shah's line), used by the protagonist for the noblewoman he rescued from bandits. Her bodyguard explicitly invokes her rank: she is 'a member of the royal family', and insulting the Shahzadi is treated as a capital offense. She states she is scheduled to enroll in the Academy of Anthus as a student and intends to attain her Masters robes, having traveled north from Pidjata. She claims the roads in Pidjata are safe for women to travel, unlike here. Whether she is connected to the protagonist's own broken childhood betrothal into the Shah's family is unstated.
- **silver road** — A surviving Rhynian road running through the mountains at high elevation, named for its silvery appearance. East from the pass's collapsed bridge it winds through the mountains toward the mine; west across the bridge it runs straight, with a tunnel carved into a distant mountain visible from it. The party follows it to reach Fort Longia. The silver color's cause is unstated (inferred: likely Rhynian make, given the fort and tunnel along its route).
- **Hani** — A bandit leader in the ruin, per another bandit's unbidden report: 'Hani keeps his journal under the third mattress on the left after one enters the barracks.' He leads at least part of the remaining bandit force, and the protagonist is tempted to read the journal. Nothing else about him is established in this batch; the bandit's claim is unverified.
- **mine shaft** — The central shaft of the prison-mine (Fort Longia): a sheer-drop pit at the heart of the facility, with a massive central support pillar linked to the shaft walls by innumerable crossway bridges, and each level just as expansive as the one above. The protagonist navigates to it by following the lit path through darkened hallways; its bottom is not visible from the top. Per Fulvia's mention (claimed), it drops fifteen miles into the crust of the planet. Tentative — depth is Fulvia's stated figure.
- **lift** — Revision: a second lift encountered — a cargo lift just past the lip of the underground river's waterfall, used to carry craft down to the bottom. Per Profectus, the party's earlier descent bypassed the falls by riding the river and landing on this lift. Suggests lifts are standard Rhynian infrastructure for vertical transport of both people and boats in the fort.
- **Jamav** — The once-per-decade festival at which the nomadic bhatak subculture gathers and settles in one location for more than a few weeks — the only time they stop moving. Tentative; only described in the narrator's overview of bhatak customs.
- **bhatak** — A subculture within Pidjata of traditionalist Samjan who insist on following the nomadic ways of their ancestors, moving constantly and only settling in one place for more than a few weeks once every decade (at the Jamav festival). Most city-dwellers consider them backwards degenerates, little better than aghtak; conversely, the bhatak consider city-dwellers traitors to their ancestors, and they often resort to petty theft, prostitution, and mischief to support themselves. Captain Darzi was born to a tribe of bhatak before joining the El-Amin guard.
- **Laborum Libertabitis** — A motto inscribed in large letters above a long, frameless black pane of glass deep inside the mine-prison (reached via the silver road), spanning a vast chamber over a chasm. The hall narrows to a narrow bridge; one side holds a long row of millennia-disused punishment cages, the other this mirror. The pane is unlike normal mirrors (not polished silver or metal-coated glass) — a black glass slab with no frame or support whose reflection appears 'somehow... off. Warped in some way.' The protagonist translates 'Laborum Libertabitis' as something akin to 'Your Labor will Set You Free' — his own hedged rendering ('if you're not mistaken'); the placement over the cages gives it a grim ironic cast. Tentative.
- **Conium** — A paralytic poison Chryssa's people use to execute non-violent criminals. Per her account (a guess, unverified): it causes paralysis starting in the legs, and eventually the poisoned individual ceases breathing; the effects last two or three days, and survival is possible if someone assists the victim's breathing (typically a throat tube and bellows). She diagnosed the plant-creature's sting as 'similar to conium poisoning' by symptoms alone (heavy legs, difficulty breathing, nausea). Claimed knowledge — Chryssa's dialogue, hedged by herself ('merely a guess at what it might cause').
- **ghoul** — Tentative: the target of the shrine spirit's contract. The protagonist tells his companions he made a contract with a fire spirit on the second level to kill a 'ghoul' whom he suspects to be an AI on the second floor — so he cannot leave the fort until the contract is completed. Neither the term's meaning in-setting nor the AI identification is confirmed; both are the protagonist's own statements (the contract mechanics and Yaratma identification are covered under the atma entry).
- **Antimage Core (Corrupted)** — The core gem recovered from a destroyed Antimage Construct. Amidst the rubble of the shattered construct lies a single gem, roughly the size of an apple, yellow in color, glowing dimly, with bits of black swirling throughout it; the GM readout logs it as '1x Antimage Core (Corrupted) Gained.' The '(Corrupted)' qualifier distinguishes it from a clean core and presumably relates to the swirling black visible in the gem — cause unstated. Per the author's Q&A answer, if a construct has a core it can be used to improve a golem the user makes, or sold for an exorbitant amount. The protagonist scoops it into the Void Cloak and flees, planning to ask Fulvia about it.
- **Permanency** — A metamagic effect per author Q&A. Permanency is required to make a creation last any length of time without constant upkeep — e.g., animating a golem works with the Animate effect alone, but the golem needs Permanency applied to persist without ongoing maintenance. Author statement (out-of-story mechanics answer).
- **attuned** — General artifact-activation mechanic ('attunement'), stated by the protagonist: 'Artifacts need to be attuned to an individual to work, at least usually. A lot of weapons don't, come to think of it.' Observed forms: the Elaudian armor attunes via a finger-prick when digits are inserted in its eye sockets (blood-link, matching the brooches' finger-prick attunement); the protagonist's own armor similarly seals itself to him on approach. So the default rule is per-individual attunement for artifacts, with weapons as a stated common exception. The 'at least usually' hedge and the weapon exception are the protagonist's own claimed understanding, not authorial rules.
- **Emergency Protocols** — Tentative entry: the AI-failsafe routine of the Rhynian facility's reactor AI, per an employee review of the AI's coding (narrated document text). If the reactor is shut off, the AI checks with the director every thirty minutes, cataloguing the reason for the interruption; if the director doesn't respond or responds unsatisfactorily, the AI activates 'Emergency Protocols' — the only known consequence being that it unlocks the AI from the AI Core. This is the likely reason the AI Core light blinked red: the AI is no longer linked to the core and could be anywhere (e.g., controlling the half-organic construct seen on the remote screen). The review recommends commending the coding analyst and investigating the AI.
- **Corrupted AI** — The enemy designation for the facility AI once unlocked from the AI Core (per Emergency Protocols): in combat it fights by controlling a construct shell from the inside — its torso sits within the shell, it wields the shell's whip-arm, and it can be wounded by damaging the torso or disabled by prying the torso free of the shell. The combat readout names it '1x Corrupted AI'. That this is the same facility AI from the ruin (the one the Emergency Protocols freed from the AI Core) is strongly implied but the text only ever says 'the AI' — the identity link is inference. Tentative.
- **Lord Treasurer** — An Anthus civic office — the city's treasurer. Per Demetrios's account (claimed), after Lord Spiros's death the city is holding public elections to determine the next Lord Treasurer; the timing implies Spiros held the office (inferred).
- **Pani's River** — The river of the death-and-birth goddess Pani into which souls pass. Per the protagonist's cultural knowledge, the normal funerary practice is burning the dead 'to speed their souls into Pani's River' — the Rhynian custom of burying their dead underground (sometimes under their homes) is noted as a deviation from this. Souls being washed by Pani in the river's waters is documented under the Pani entry's author Q&A cosmology; this is the lay version of that belief.
